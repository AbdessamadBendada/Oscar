2. When we work on behalf of your employer

When a client asks us to run a diagnostic, survey or interviews among its employees, the client decides why that happens. We then process the data on the client's behalf, as a processor under a data processing agreement. The client is the controller and its own privacy notice applies.

Before a diagnostic starts, participants receive a short explanation of what is collected, who sees what and how results are reported. We report results in aggregated form and never show results for groups smaller than [five] people. Individual answers are not passed on to your employer, unless you take part in a named role, such as the founder, and are told so in advance.

Questions about such a diagnostic can go to your employer or to us. We will help either way.

3. What we collect, why, and on what basis

3.1 Visiting our website

Data
IP address, browser type and settings, device type, pages visited, date and time of the visit and the referring page, recorded in server log files.
Why
To deliver the website securely, detect misuse and fix errors.
Legal basis
Legitimate interest (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR) in a secure, working website.
Kept for
No longer than [30 days], unless needed to investigate a security incident.

For cookies and website statistics, see our Cookie Policy.

3.2 Contacting us or booking a call

Data
Name, email address, company, job title, your message and anything else you choose to share. When you book a call: the date, time and your answers to the booking questions.
Why
To reply, plan a conversation and assess whether we are a good fit.
Legal basis
Steps you asked for before a possible contract (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR) and our legitimate interest in responding to business enquiries (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR).
Kept for
12 months after our last contact, unless it leads to an engagement (see 3.5).

3.3 The Noise Tax Calculator

Data
The calculator works with figures about your organisation, such as headcount, average salary and time estimates. These are company figures, not personal data. If you ask for your report by email, we also process your name, email address, company and your inputs.
Why
To calculate your result, send you the report and follow up once on that result.
Legal basis
Steps you asked for (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR); legitimate interest for the single follow-up (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR).
Kept for
12 months after our last contact.

[Confirm before publishing: if you use the calculator without requesting a report, your inputs stay in your browser and we do not store them.]

3.4 The Free OPS Diagnostic and other diagnostics

When we run a diagnostic for your employer, section 2 applies. When you complete a diagnostic yourself, for example as a founder preparing for a conversation with us:

Data
Name, email address, company, role and your answers.
Why
To analyse your results and discuss them with you.
Legal basis
Steps you asked for before a possible contract (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR).
Kept for
12 months after our last contact, or as engagement data if we start working together.

After a diagnostic, we strip out everything that could identify a person or an organisation and keep only anonymised, aggregated figures to build benchmarks. That anonymisation step rests on our legitimate interest in improving our methods (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR). Anonymised data is no longer personal data.

3.5 Clients and engagements

Data
Contact details of the people we work with, meeting notes, correspondence, Deliverables, participant lists and billing details.
Why
To plan and deliver the engagement, communicate and invoice.
Legal basis
Performance of the contract (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR). Where our contract is with your organisation rather than with you personally, our legitimate interest in carrying it out (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR). Legal obligation for financial records (Article 6(1)(c) GDPR).
Kept for
Invoices and financial records: 7 years, as Dutch tax law requires (Article 52 of the General State Taxes Act, AWR). Other engagement files: [2 years] after the engagement ends, unless we need them longer to handle a claim.

3.6 Workshops, keynotes and events

Data
Name, organisation, role and email address. If you choose to share them: dietary or accessibility needs. Photos and video of the event.
Why
To organise the event, give you access and make it accessible and safe. With your consent, to use photos and video in our communication.
Legal basis
Performance of the contract or legitimate interest (Article 6(1)(b) and (f) GDPR). Dietary or accessibility needs that reveal health or religious information: your explicit consent (Article 9(2)(a) GDPR). Recognisable photos and video used for promotion: your consent, or at public keynotes our legitimate interest, with clear notice beforehand and an easy way to stay out of the picture.
Kept for
Attendee details: [3 months] after the event. Dietary and accessibility needs: deleted within one month after the event. Photos and video: until you withdraw consent or we stop using them.

3.7 Recording and transcribing calls and sessions

We only record or transcribe a call or session if we tell you in advance and you agree. This includes AI-generated meeting notes. Recordings and raw transcripts are deleted within [90 days]. Notes that become part of a Deliverable follow the retention period for engagement files. Legal basis: your consent (Article 6(1)(a) GDPR), which you can withdraw at any moment during or after the call.

3.8 Newsletter and updates

Data
Name, email address, company and [whether you open or click our emails, if your newsletter tool measures this].
Why
To send you updates and articles you asked for.
Legal basis
Your consent (Article 6(1)(a) GDPR). Existing clients may receive updates about similar services without prior consent, as Article 11.7 of the Dutch Telecommunications Act (Telecommunicatiewet) allows, always with an unsubscribe link.
Kept for
Until you unsubscribe. We then remove your details within 30 days and keep only your email address on a suppression list, so we never email you again by mistake.

Data
Name, delivery and billing address, email address, phone number for delivery, order details and payment status. We never see or store your full card or bank details; the payment provider handles those.
Why
To process and deliver your order, handle withdrawals and returns, and provide customer service.
Legal basis
Performance of the contract (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR) and legal obligation for financial records (Article 6(1)(c) GDPR).

3.10 Social media

When you interact with our LinkedIn or Instagram pages, the platform processes your data under its own privacy policy. For the page statistics these platforms give us, the platform and OPS Detox are joint controllers; we only see aggregated statistics and what you share publicly. We use messages you send us to reply to you, on the basis of our legitimate interest (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR).

3.11 Legal obligations and claims

We may use personal data to comply with a legal obligation (Article 6(1)(c) GDPR) or to establish, exercise or defend a legal claim, on the basis of our legitimate interest (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR).

4. What we never do

We do not sell or rent personal data, and we do not share it with third parties for their own marketing. We do not take decisions about you based solely on automated processing, and we do not build profiles of individual visitors.

6. Transfers outside the European Economic Area

Some providers, such as large software companies, may process data outside the European Economic Area, for example in the United States. We only allow this when the European Commission has recognised the country as adequate, including transfers to companies certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, or when the provider has signed the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses with appropriate additional safeguards. You can ask us for a copy of the relevant safeguards.

7. How we protect your data

We use encrypted connections (HTTPS), two-factor authentication on our accounts, encrypted devices and strict access control: only Oscar de Grauw and, where needed, the associates working on an engagement can reach personal data. We choose providers who meet the GDPR and bind them by contract.

If a personal data breach occurs, we report it to the Dutch Data Protection Authority within 72 hours where the law requires, and inform you directly if it poses a high risk to you.

8. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have these rights:

Access
Ask which personal data we hold about you and receive a copy.
Rectification
Have incorrect or incomplete data corrected.
Erasure
Have your data deleted, unless we must keep it by law or need it for a legal claim.
Restriction
Have the processing of your data limited in certain situations.
Data portability
Receive data you gave us in a structured, machine-readable format, where processing rests on consent or contract.
Objection
Object to processing based on legitimate interest. You can always object to direct marketing, with no reason needed.
Withdraw consent
Withdraw consent at any time. This does not affect processing that took place before.

Send your request to info@opsdetox.nl. We respond within one month. For complex or numerous requests we may extend this by two months, and if so we tell you within the first month.

We may ask you to confirm your identity, for example by replying from the email address we know. We will not ask for a copy of your passport or ID card unless there is no other way. If we do, you may shield your photo and citizen service number (BSN).

If you believe we handle your data incorrectly, you can file a complaint with the Dutch Data Protection Authority (Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens) at www.autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl. We would appreciate hearing from you first, but you do not have to.

9. Children

Our website and services are not aimed at children under 16. If we learn that a child under 16 has given us personal data without the consent of a parent or guardian, we delete it.

10. Other websites
